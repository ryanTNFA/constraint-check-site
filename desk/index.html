Why this exists

Growth is how countries leave poverty, and aid, behind.

Two centuries ago most people everywhere were poor (Maddison Project). Where incomes rose for decades, poverty fell and countries moved from aid to trade. The New Foreign Assistance holds that sustained growth is the only proven cure for extreme poverty.

Find what holds growth back before spending.

In each economy a few problems hold private investment back the most: unreliable power in one, insecure land titles or a slow road to the port in another. Fixing one of those lets investment follow. Fixing one that isn’t holding anything back changes little.

A public first read, for any country.

This site applies the method behind the Millennium Challenge Corporation’s constraints analyses to public data for 217 economies. MCC’s own analyses take months of fieldwork; this is an automated first read, often with thin data. It is a starting point for people on the ground, not a verdict.

How to read a country page (five minutes)

Economist new to the method? Learn it in eight lessons on these pages, about three hours, worked on Kenya.

Have a program idea? Pick a country, then use “Test a program idea” on its Bottom line: Constraint Check packs the evidence into one file for the AI you already use.

  1. Bottom line. Three lists: what you can stop arguing about, what deserves a closer look, and what the public data can’t settle, with what would. “Probably not” is weaker than “ruled out”. Each line has a “Why?” link.
  2. Areas. The decision tree and each area’s evidence card: what points each way, what would change the rating, and what to ask in two days in-country.
  3. Test a program idea (on the Bottom line). Describe it and download one file with the evidence and instructions; your own AI assistant does the assessment. Constraint Check runs no AI and never says “fund this”.
  4. For analysts: Charts and the Heat map hold every chart, test and number.

How we test it. A pre-registered retrospective against 57 published diagnoses (MCC constraints analyses and Harvard Growth Lab growth diagnostics), run on data truncated to what each analysis could have seen, with the pass and fail lines written down before any comparison. Result, 3 November 2026: the tool wrongly ruled out 9 of 175 constraints the analysts found binding (on the sealed holdout, 3 of 75), flagged 13, and said “cannot tell” for 86 percent of areas. It commits so rarely that the test could neither pass nor fail its own bar: by its own rule the primary result is inconclusive. No scoring rule was tuned to the analysts’ findings. The full results are published whatever they say. Treat every rating as a starting point.
