Learning the method · lesson 1 of 8

How a country gets read

The growth-diagnostics method, walked once on one country, Kenya, as the tool reads it. Nine short steps; scroll. About fifteen minutes. It teaches the desk half of the method: what the data can say before anyone goes to the country.

Rubric 0.2.1 · public data snapshot … · the numbers below are the tool's reading of Kenya on that date.

1 · The question

What limits private investment in this country, now?

Not "what is wrong with the economy". Many things are wrong with every economy. The question is which one or few problems bind: the ones that, relaxed, would let investment follow, while fixing the others changes little.

The one analogy worth keeping is a doctor's differential diagnosis. A patient with a fever has a hundred possible causes. The doctor does not treat all hundred; they run the tests that rule causes out, narrow to a few, and treat those. The method does the same with the standard explanations for weak investment: it works by elimination.

2 · Surprise is the unit of evidence

Not the level: the gap from what income predicts

Almost everything improves with income. Richer countries have fewer bribes, more schooling, better roads. So a number read against the world says mostly how rich a country is. The method reads each number against what a country at the same income would show, and counts the gap in standard deviations.

4 · The first fork

Costly finance, or low returns?

The tree's first question. If investment is low because finance is scarce, finance should be dear: a high real lending rate, credit not growing. If finance is cheap and investment is still low, the problem is on the other side: the returns to investing are low, or cannot be kept. A price tells you which side the shortage is on. A quantity alone cannot.

The word "provisional" matters. The top split is a reading of three numbers, not a verdict; the branches below have to agree with it.

5 · Four tests on one area

Finance in Kenya: two tests pull against each other

A binding constraint shows in four ways. Its price is high. Investment moves when it moves. Firms pay to work around it. And activities that need little of it do unusually well (the camels and hippos test: in a desert, camels thrive). One test can be fooled. The method asks several kinds of evidence to agree.

Every area, with its rating and, where it can't tell, why

The tool walks the whole tree. Most areas end as "can't tell", and each one says which kind: only one kind of evidence speaks; the measure isn't connected yet; the data are too old; or the evidence is mixed. That is not a shrug. Each kind sends fieldwork somewhere different.

7 · The asymmetry

Flagging is easy. Clearing is hard.

The tool's verdicts are used to stop work. A wrong "look closer" costs a field visit. A wrong "ruled out" sends attention away from a constraint that binds, and that is the costliest mistake the method can make. So the ladders are not symmetric. A "voting family" below is one independent source of price or workaround evidence; quantities and indices inform but do not vote.

8 · The three lists

What the page says at the end

Every country page ends with three lists, each for a different decision. Stop arguing about: areas whose evidence leans away, so a program should not be built around them without new evidence. Look closer: where a field visit, a diagnostic or a threshold program should start. Can't settle: what to measure first.

9 · Check yourself

Why does the tool clear an area more carefully than it flags one?

Show the answer

Because its verdicts are used to stop work. A false clear sends attention away from a constraint that binds, so "ruled out" needs a low price, no workarounds, five voting families and three agreeing tests, while one strong test can flag. "Probably not" sits at a flag's low bar, so it never closes a debate alone. The Mindbook puts it as the asymmetry of errors.

The eight lessons

About three hours in all, on the pages that already exist, worked on Kenya with one card borrowed from Senegal. Each lesson's bar carries its task, a check question and the next step. Nothing is stored; the lesson travels in the link.

    The exit test

    After lesson 8: a second country, drawn from Ghana, Bangladesh and Uganda, with the verdicts hidden. Sixty minutes, no help. For the top split and each of the thirteen areas: your verdict, which tests speak, which is missing, and for every "can't tell", the next step. Then reveal, and write a line on each disagreement. A pass needs no false clear anywhere, ten of thirteen verdicts matching or soundly argued, four in five explanations naming the right tests, and a top split read from the price of finance.

    The tool is a check on your reading, not the answer: a disagreement argued in the method's terms counts.
