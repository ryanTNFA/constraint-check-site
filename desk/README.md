# The Desk (static web UI)

The Desk renders the Scorecards the engine writes to `desk/data/` and re-runs the engine's aggregation in the browser when the user edits the comparator set. No build step, no npm packages: plain HTML, one CSS file, ES modules served as-is. External resources are D3 7.9.0 from cdnjs and two Google Fonts (Source Sans 3, Source Serif 4); everything else is local.

## Run it

From the project root (`cc/`):

```
python -m http.server 8766 --directory .      # or the "desk" launch config in .claude/launch.json
```

Then open <http://localhost:8766/desk/> (picker) or <http://localhost:8766/desk/country.html?iso=SLE> (Snapshot). The site fetches, by absolute path, `/registry/*.json`, `/desk/data/*.json`, `/engine/src/scorecard.mjs` (optional: comparator recompute) and `/synth/narratives/{iso}.json` (optional: narrative text on the node cards; its 404 is the one expected console error).

To look at another output folder without copying it, add `?data=<absolute path under the server root>`, for example `country.html?iso=SLE&data=/out/desk-data`. The picker carries the parameter into its links.

## Data

`desk/data/` holds the engine CLI's output for all 217 economies (`node engine/cli.mjs --snapshot data/snapshot --registry registry --out out --desk desk/data --generated-at ...`): `index.json`, `matrix.json`, `countries.json` and `{iso3}.json` (the Scorecard plus six series for charts), regenerated by every engine run (engine 0.1.3, rubric 0.1.6 as of 29 October 2026). The files are regenerated by the CLI, never edited by hand. `desk/dev/mock.mjs` was the mock generator of the first Desk build; it targets rubric 0.1.0 (`comovement.sign_consistency_threshold`, the `low_level` classification, every family voting on the price test) and now refuses to run against a later rubric with a message pointing at the CLI. The pages still show a banner if they ever see `meta.mock` or a mock engine version.

The Desk reads these fields (engine spec sections 4 and 5 plus the engine's additions through rubric 0.1.2; `engine/README.md` section 11 lists them all):

* `meta` (`snapshot_id`, `snapshot_year`, `rubric_version`, `engine_version`, `generated_at`, `active_comparator_set`, `seed`), `country`.
* `growth_problem`: `statement`, `classification`, G1 to G12 through a small normalizer (`gNorm`), plus the v0.1.2 window spans `G1.window_10y`, `window_20y`, `years_10y` (shown on the G1 cell when the observed years are shorter than the window).
* `top_split`: `row`, `label`, `reading`, `rate_source`, `stats` (`real_rate_t`, `credit_growth`, `investment_t`, `credit_depth_t`, `net_cashflow_banks`), `classification` (`rate`, `credit`, `investment`, `credit_depth`), `thresholds`, `missing`.
* `comparators[set]`: `rule`, `members`, `distances`, `excluded`, `growth`, `reason_empty`.
* `indicators[]`: `available`, `value_ref`, `year_ref`, `n_ref`, `icr`, `peer_gap[set]` (with `ties`, `tied`, `insufficient`), `signal_by_set`, `signal`, `signal_icr`, `recency_mult`, `weight`, `stale_value/year`, `class`, `class_registry`, `class_note` (regime downgrade), `formula`, `caveat`, `fallback_for`, `suppressed`, `suppressed_note`.
* `comovement[]`: `x_source`, `y`, `lag`, `expected_sign`, `agree`, `pairs`, `consistency`, `p_value`, `direction`, `slope`, `signal`, `test_rule`, `years`.
* `nodes[]`: `tier`, `tier_subreason` (`data_gap`, `stale`, `connector_not_built`, `no_ab_family`, `no_ab_price_family`, `registry_coverage`, `conflicting`, `weak`, `composite_only`, `main`, `alt`), `tier_reason`, `blockers`, `N`, `N_weights`, `C`, `E` (voting families), `E_informing`, `R`, `F`, `AB_count`, `AB_pos`, `tests[k]` (`T`, `families`, `informing`, `not_observed`, `weight_mass`, `shrunk`), `price_test_note`, `informing`, `informing_dissent`, `families[]` (`votes`, `vote_note`, `readings`), `tier_by_set`, `fragility`, `load_bearing`, `connectors_missing`, `sub_nodes`, `sub_node_tiers`, `rollup_from`, `top_split_override`, `top_split_override_row`, `top_split_note`, `scored`.
* `ranking`: `ordered`, `scores`, `modal_rank`, `p_top3`, `p_ranked`, `not_resampled`, `bootstrap_draws`, `too_many_likely_binding`; `sensitivity`: `tier_by_set`, `load_bearing_families`, `threshold_sensitivity`; `statements`; `series`.

## Design notes

The TNFA system (see "Visual system" below): `bg-soft` page, `ink` text, one amber accent for interactive elements, square corners; tier colours are used only for tiers, and the accent is never a tier colour. Newsreader for headings and prose, Libertinus Serif Display for the tracked uppercase eyebrows and buttons, Inter for data and controls, IBM Plex Mono for table headers. Tabular figures throughout and a true minus sign. No gradients and no icon set; one soft shadow (`--elev`), for the drawer, menus and tooltips only (visual-upgrade PRD, V1). An editorial type scale: the headline (`h1`, up to 4.25rem on the home page), a standfirst (1.375rem), body at 1.0625rem, and three small sizes kept apart (`--fs-eyebrow`, `--fs-caption`, `--fs-label`). Panels sit on the page under a single rule rather than in bordered boxes; borders stay on interactive cards. On the home map every "can't tell" is one quiet tint (`--tier-cannot-tint`), outlined for a data gap; the three kinds keep their hatches in lists, chips and cards. `tests/test_palette.py` checks the tokens: text pairs 4.5:1, marks 3:1, the amber and crimson tiers apart under colour-vision deficiency, both dark-theme copies equal. The tree fits a 1140px desktop without horizontal page scroll and scrolls horizontally inside its own panel on phones. Every number on screen comes from a value in the data file or the registry; the rubric parameters shown (thresholds, staleness limit, bootstrap draws, the credit-depth cut-off) are read from `registry/params.json`.

## Accessibility

Skip link, landmark sections, visible focus styles, keyboard-navigable tree (Tab, Enter, Space, arrows, Escape), dialog semantics on the drawer with a focus trap and focus return (focus is moved out before `aria-hidden` is set, so Chrome no longer warns), `aria-live` on the picker count and the Proposal Check output, AA-contrast token pairs in both themes (checked by hand: text on page 15:1, muted 5.6:1, accent 6.5:1, white on red 6.5:1, dark text on amber 7:1, dark text on light grey 10:1).

## Visual system (TNFA), October 2026

The Desk follows the TNFA Design System (https://claude.ai/artifact/FGtwDL393QuVYodATMiRLR): `bg-soft` ground, `ink` text, one amber accent (`amber-mid` for text and fills), square corners, the TNFA logo in the header, and the TNFA fonts served from `fonts/` (no Google Fonts). Newsreader sets headings and readouts, Libertinus Serif Display the uppercase eyebrows and buttons, Inter the data and controls, IBM Plex Mono the table headers.

- **Data palette:** charts draw from role tokens in `css/desk.css`: `--chart-focus` (the economy, amber), `--chart-peer` and `--chart-peer-fill` (comparators, navy), `--chart-fit` and `--chart-band` (the reference line and band), `--chart-other` (every other economy) and `--chart-binding` (the binding side, crimson). Verdict tiers form a diverging scale from navy (ruled out) through a neutral hatch (cannot tell) to crimson (likely binding). The palette is also published in the design system (tokens `chart-*`, `tier-*`, `control-border`; section "Data visualization").
- **Checks:** every text pair clears 4.5:1 and every mark 3:1 in both themes, except the grey background points; amber and crimson stay distinguishable under colour-vision deficiency (ΔE 15.4).
- **Dark theme:** a tool-only extension (the website has none) on a navy ground derived from TNFA `banner`, with a lighter amber; the logo sits on a light plate.

## Evidence page (C1 to C4, C7, C9), October 2026

`/desk/evidence.html?iso=KEN&node=N3` opens on a node. A **left rail** in Mindbook order (growth record, what firms say, N0, then the finance, social-returns, government-failure and market-failure branches, with N11's sub-nodes indented) carries each node's verdict tier; on phones it becomes a scrolling strip. The view for the rail entry sits at the top (code in `js/evidence_node.mjs`):

- **C9, evidence strip (a node):** every indicator mapped to the node (a roll-up includes its sub-nodes), one row each, grouped by test, at the verdict layer's own distance from what income and structure predict (`icr.t`), turned by the indicator's direction so the binding side is always right. Filled circle A, filled square B, hollow circle C, hollow diamond D; dotted lines at 0.75 and 1.5 sd; the rank among the economy and its comparators and whether the family votes are on the right. Below: the co-movement test, tests not computed, and indicators with no value in the window. These are the snapshot's numbers (`stripRows` in `js/evidence_math.mjs`). Click a row to chart that indicator below.
- **C4, growth record:** GDP per capita (constant US$) indexed to 100 in a base year (`#since=`1960, 1980, 1990, 2000) on a log scale against the comparators' median and middle half, with the engine's peak; annual growth bars beneath with the comparators' median growth and the engine's break year and before/after means. The two panels are one SVG for export.
- **C7, what firms say:** Enterprise Survey obstacle shares, biggest obstacle or major constraint (`#measure=major`): the latest wave (filled), the wave before (dashed outline) and the comparators' median of their latest waves (tick, three or more needed), with the perception and survivor caveat. Click a row to chart that obstacle below.
- **Both ways to the verdict layer:** the node header shows the tier and links to `country.html?iso=…&node=…`, which now opens that node's card; each node card links back here.

Beneath, `ind=` picks the indicator for C1 to C3 (a rail pick chooses the node's first class A/B indicator with data). `/desk/evidence.html?iso=KEN&ind=FR.INR.RINR` draws any indicator against log GDP per capita for every economy, with the fitted line, a ±1 robust-standard-deviation band, the country highlighted and its comparators labelled by ISO code (evidence-viz-prd chart C1).

- **Settings** (kept in the URL): economy, indicator (grouped by tree node, each labelled with its class, e.g. "class A, price"), comparator set (structural by default), fit (linear Huber by default, LOWESS optional), data as of a past year, a **Custom** comparator set (`#set=custom`: only the economies you add, as published charts do), economies added to the comparators (`#add=`; the old `#label=` still reads). Added economies count as comparators everywhere: C1 labels, C2 bars, rank, median and band, and C3 lines (their lines stay when the others collapse to the band); the wording becomes "structural and added comparators". The verdict layer still uses the preset set. They sit in a control bar that stays pinned to the top of the window while the charts scroll; at 900 px and below it collapses to a one-line summary with a Change button.
- **Back to the latest:** while "Data as of" is set to a past year, the control bar outlines that field and shows a "↩ 2026" button ("Back to 2026" in the collapsed phone bar). It is hidden when the latest year has no value for the economy and indicator, since going back would only empty the charts.
- **Coverage:** `data/coverage/{iso3}.json` (written by the CLI: `[first_year, last_year, n_years]` per indicator) greys out indicators with no data for the economy and marks those whose data end before the window ("latest 2005"). When a chart is empty, it says which years the economy has and offers a "Show data as of …" button for the nearest as-of year with both a point and a line (`viableYears` in `js/evidence_math.mjs`).
- **Window:** the indicator's own `reference_window_years` (20 for slow-moving series such as returns to schooling), else the snapshot window (12), as in the engine.
- **Direction:** for indicators with a direction, C1 tints the binding side of the line faintly in the binding colour and labels both sides ("▲ Above the line: toward binding"); C3 labels higher and lower the same way. The C1 readout says whether the distance from the line points toward or away from binding, or reads as neutral, on the verdict layer's thresholds (`icr.signal_thresholds`: 0.75 sd a lean, 1.5 sd strong), both on income alone and as adjusted by the verdict layer. The C2 readout says which side of the comparator median points toward binding. Context indicators (direction 0) get none of this.
- **Indicator classes:** the subtitle states the indicator's class and verdict weight, and a collapsible key under the control bar explains A to D and X (Workbook 4.6).
- **Hover tips** use the page's surface and text tokens, so they read in dark mode.
- **Numbers:** the linear line is fitted the engine's way (winsorized at the 1st and 99th percentiles, Huber IRLS) on income alone; the readout also prints the verdict layer's residual adjusted for income and structure. The chart math is in `js/evidence_math.mjs` and tested in `engine/test/evidence_math.test.mjs`.
- **Data:** `data/panel/{indicator}.json` (every economy's annual values) and `data/fits.json` (the engine's fits), both written by `node engine/cli.mjs`.
- **C2, against its comparators:** horizontal bars of the economy and its comparators (same reference rule: the average of the latest three years in the window), sorted, with the comparator median (dashed) and middle half (shaded). Each label carries its latest year; bars older than the staleness limit are dashed outlines. The readout gives rank, the gap to the median in units (percentage points for percentages), and the gap in interquartile ranges.
- **C3, over time:** annual lines over 10, 20 or 30 years (`#yrs=`), in levels or as change since each line's first year (`#view=change`), with each comparator as a thin labelled line or only the median and middle-half band (`#peerlines=band`; the band is drawn where at least three comparators have data). Series with values for fewer than half the years (survey waves) are drawn dotted with points. The readout gives the start and end values, any peak or trough in between that outweighs the end-to-end change, and fitted trends per year (dense series only).
- **Exports:** each chart has its own PNG (1600 px wide), SVG and CSV. The C2 CSV includes the median and quartiles; the C3 CSV includes the band by year.
- **Links:** "Evidence charts" in the Snapshot header; "See the evidence charts for this node" in each node card.

## Year player (charts 1 and 2), October 2026

PRD phase 3 (section 7.1): the "data as of" year advancing on its own. The control bar above chart 1 (`js/player.mjs`) has Play/Pause, a year scrubber (arrow keys step a year), a speed choice and "Back to the static chart".

- **Chart 1 becomes a bubble chart:** log income on x, bubbles sized by population (`SP.POP.TOTL`), the income line and its band gliding as the world changes, the economy's path traced in amber from the first year, and the year large in the background. The economy's label carries its distance from the line in every frame.
- **Chart 2 becomes a bar race:** bars re-sort as ranks change; the comparator median and middle half move with them.
- **Honest motion:** axes are fixed from all frames (`playerFrames`, `playerDomains` and `raceFrames` in `js/evidence_math.mjs`, tested). Points use the static charts' reference rule; points older than the staleness limit stay hollow; economies fade in and out with their data and are never interpolated. Comparators are today's set, and the player says so.
- **Every paused frame is a chart:** pausing, or letting go of the scrubber, sets the page's "data as of" year, so the readouts, footnotes, other charts and the link all describe that year. During playback the readouts under charts 1 and 2 are dimmed, because they still describe the last paused year.
- **Motion on request:** nothing moves until Play. With reduced motion set in the operating system, playback steps from year to year without gliding.
- **Exports** of charts 1 and 2 while the player is open save the frame on screen. Video or GIF export is PRD phase 5.

## Plain-language pass, 29 October 2026

Two persona walkthroughs (a policy officer and an economist) found that the Snapshot spoke engine notation, buried its answer and could be cited as "fund this" or as a false rule-out. The fixes, and the changes proposed for the Evidence and Summary pages, are in `docs/ux-clarity-pass.md`. In short:

- **One vocabulary.** `js/card.mjs` exports the plain-language helpers every Snapshot surface uses: plain tier names (`PLAIN_TIER`: worth a closer look, probably not the main constraint, unclear: …), readings with their years and peer rank (`factText`, `refSpan` for three-year means such as "2022–24"), the plain card paragraph (`plainSummary`), what would move a rating (`whatWouldMove`), field questions (`FIELD_QUESTIONS`, to move into the registry), the three statements as data (`bottomLine`) and the fragile/conflict note (`fcsNote`, from `registry/static_fcs.json`). The engine's rule strings stay one click away under "Rule detail (for analysts)".
- **Order.** Snapshot: bottom line, check a program idea, tree, growth question, then a collapsed "For analysts" group (first decision, ranking, comparators, provenance), then the memo. In-page links scroll without touching the URL hash, which holds the comparator set and overrides.
- **Licensed to say stop, not go.** "You can stop arguing about" is reserved for ruled out; "probably not" has its own heading with the counter-signal and the evidence years; Proposal Check paragraphs never say a constraint binds, say "neither supports nor rules out" for unclear areas, and point to the areas the evidence does flag.
- **Tree.** One row per leaf so no edge crosses another branch; micro risk's sub-areas are stacked under it; captions read "14 sources · 2021–2025".

## Test 4 and C12, camels and hippos (29 October 2026)

- **What it shows.** Under N0, N3, N4 and N6, "What thrives, and what it needs" draws one panel per intensity vector.
  - Each dot is an HS 4-digit export product.
  - x is how much of the factor the product needs: its percentile among products, on US-based intensities.
  - y is how much more the economy exports of that product than economies at its income level, measured as the residual of ln(1 + RCA) from a per-product fit on log income.
  - The line is the fitted tilt: the economy's average residual plus θ times intensity, so it slopes the way θ does.
  - θ is the slope per standard deviation of intensity: the Rajan-Zingales statistic of Workbook 4.5. Its standard errors are clustered by source industry (HS2 chapter for the time vector).
  - The panel subtitle gives θ, its standard error, and the economy's rank among about 180 economies (1 = most camels).
- **Wording.** The reading names a pattern, never a verdict:
  - "camels pattern" when θ < 0;
  - "hippos pattern" when θ > 0;
  - "no clear pattern" when θ is within two standard errors of zero.

  For now every vector is shown only: the verdict layer does not count test 4 until it passes a placebo-based kill criterion (rubric changelog, 29 October 2026; PRD section 18, E5).
- **Data.**
  - `desk/data/camels/products.json` holds product codes, names and intensity percentiles per vector.
  - `desk/data/camels/<ISO3>.json` holds θ, its standard error, rank and the product residuals for the latest year.
  - Both are written by `ingest/camels.py`. The chart does not follow the as-of year.
