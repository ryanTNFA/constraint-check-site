Growth-Grade Evidence

Evidence counts for development when it runs all the way to growth. The first link in that chain is showing that the thing proposed addresses what actually holds private investment back in that economy.

Why a standard

Over twenty years, randomized trials have taught the field a great deal about what moves a village-level outcome. They cannot, by their nature, tell a government or a donor what holds back a national economy, and the field has let “evidence-based” come to mean “measured well” rather than “on the path to growth”. This is not an argument against trials. It is an argument about what they show. A trial can establish that an intervention moves an outcome. Whether that outcome is on the path to growth in that economy is a different question, and it comes first.

A well-run trial that moves an outcome nobody has connected to growth is evidence about measurement. It is not yet evidence about poverty.

The three links

An intervention is evidence-based for development when each link below holds, in order. Most proposals never state the first.

  1. From the program to what binds.Is there a reason to think this addresses one of the few things that hold private investment back here, rather than one of the many that do not? This is the growth test, and it is what the tool speaks to.
  2. From the constraint to investment.If that constraint eases, does private investment respond? The growth-diagnostics literature carries this link: a binding constraint is one whose shadow price is high and whose relief moves investment.
  3. From investment to growth.Does the investment it unlocks raise the economy’s income for long enough to matter? The growth record, not the program’s own results, answers this.

What the tool speaks to

The first link, from public data, as a first read

That is the first link only. It does not trace a program to growth, and from public data it never will.
